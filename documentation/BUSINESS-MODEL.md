# 1. Purpose

This document defines the business model of FrontDesk.

It describes:

- who FrontDesk serves,
- what value it provides,
- how value is delivered,
- potential revenue models,
- customer acquisition,
- retention mechanisms,
- cost structure,
- expansion opportunities,
- and the relationship between the v0.1 wedge and the long-term AI Business OS vision.

This document does not define detailed product requirements or technical implementation.

---

# 2. Business Model Thesis

FrontDesk should not primarily monetize the creation of a website.

The long-term business model should monetize the ongoing digital operation of a business.

The strategic progression is:

    Website
       ↓
    Digital Presence
       ↓
    Customer Interaction
       ↓
    Business Operations
       ↓
    Automation
       ↓
    AI
       ↓
    Business OS

The more operationally valuable FrontDesk becomes, the stronger the potential recurring revenue model becomes.

---

# 3. Core Business Proposition

FrontDesk's initial proposition is:

> Bring your existing business information to FrontDesk and turn it into a digital business presence.

The long-term proposition is:

> FrontDesk helps small businesses establish, operate, automate, and grow their digital business.

# 11. Potential Revenue Model 1 — Freemium

A free plan could provide basic functionality.

Example:

## Free

- Business profile
- Basic catalog
- Basic website
- QR
- Basic WhatsApp connection
- Limited customization

Purpose:

> Reduce adoption friction.

---

# 12. Potential Revenue Model 2 — Subscription

Paid plans could provide advanced capabilities.

Potential structure:

### Starter

For small businesses beginning their digital journey.

### Growth

For businesses that actively use FrontDesk for customer interaction and marketing.

### Pro

For businesses requiring advanced automation, analytics, and AI.

The exact pricing should not be finalized until customer research and willingness-to-pay validation are performed.

# 19. Initial Monetization Recommendation

For v0.1:

> **Do not optimize the product around revenue yet.**

First validate:

1. Business import
2. Successful activation
3. Customer interaction
4. Owner updates
5. Repeat usage
6. 30-day retention

After evidence of recurring value, test willingness to pay.

# 20. Customer Acquisition Strategy

The initial acquisition strategy should focus on businesses that already have some digital presence but have room for improvement.

Potential acquisition channels:

### Direct outreach

Approach local businesses directly.

Example:

> "We can turn your existing menu and business information into a mobile-friendly digital presence."

---

### Existing website migration

Target businesses with outdated websites.

Offer:

> "Bring your existing website to FrontDesk."

---

### Social media businesses

Target businesses operating primarily through Instagram and WhatsApp.

Offer:

> "Turn your Instagram business into a proper digital storefront."

---

### QR adoption

Target businesses that already use printed menus or QR codes.

Offer:

> "Turn your QR menu into a complete digital business page."

---

### Referral

Existing businesses refer other businesses.

Potential future incentive:

> Refer another business → receive plan credits or premium features.

# 21. Acquisition Wedge

> **Bring your business here. We'll make it digital.**

# 27. Zero-Cost Development Strategy

During initial development:

- prefer open-source software,
- use local development,
- use free infrastructure tiers,
- avoid unnecessary paid APIs,
- avoid unnecessary SaaS dependencies,
- use mock integrations where official credentials are unavailable.

The architecture should allow paid services to be introduced later without rewriting the entire product.

---

# 28. Vendor Independence

The product should separate business logic from third-party services.

For example:

    FrontDesk
       ↓
    Messaging Interface
       ↓
    Provider Adapter
       ↓
    WhatsApp / Other Provider

Similarly:

    FrontDesk AI Interface
       ↓
    AI Provider Adapter
       ↓
    Local / Cloud Model

This allows providers to change without redesigning the entire business system.

# 31. Business Model Risks

## Risk 1 — Free users never convert

If the free product provides enough value, users may never pay.

### Response

Keep premium capabilities tied to meaningful business outcomes rather than artificial restrictions.

---

## Risk 2 — Infrastructure costs exceed revenue

AI and messaging can become expensive.

### Response

Use quotas, provider abstraction, caching, local AI where practical, and cost monitoring.

---

## Risk 3 — Customers only need a website

If customers do not adopt operational features, the recurring SaaS opportunity becomes weaker.

### Response

Validate retention before building a large operations suite.

---

## Risk 4 — High customer acquisition cost

Small businesses can be difficult to acquire individually.

### Response

Explore:

- agencies,
- referrals,
- partnerships,
- vertical-specific acquisition,
- reseller models,
- and self-serve onboarding.

The immediate objective is not maximizing revenue.
